Know what to look for

Nine species. One shared home.

An invasive species is a nonnative organism that causes harm. These quick clues are a starting point: follow the official guides for photos, look-alikes and reporting instructions.

Showing 9 species

01Wetland plant

Phragmites

Look forTall reeds with feathery seed heads.

Why it mattersDense stands crowd out native wetland plants.

How you can help

Clean mud and plant fragments from boots and gear. Native phragmites also exists; ask an expert to confirm identification.

02Aquatic animal

Rusty crayfish

Look forRust-colored patches on each side; large claws.

Why it mattersCompetes with native crayfish and consumes aquatic plants.

How you can help

Never release bait or aquarium animals into a lake or river.

Sea lamprey

Look forAn eel-like fish with a round, toothed suction mouth.

Why it mattersAttaches to large fish and feeds on their body fluids.

How you can help

Learn how experts use traps, barriers and targeted treatments. Michigan also has native lamprey species.

04Aquatic animal

Spiny water flea

Look forTiny crustacean with a long, barbed tail spine.

Why it mattersCompetes with small fish for food and collects on fishing lines.

How you can help

Inspect and clean fishing lines, nets and other wet gear before moving between lakes.

Make your next outing count

Clean. Drain. Dry.

Invasive species can travel with our boats, fishing gear and outdoor equipment. Build these habits into every trip.

01 / Clean

Remove plants, mud and debris from boats, trailers, boots and gear before leaving an access site.

02 / Drain

Drain water from boats and equipment, including live wells and bilges, before transporting them.

03 / Dry

Allow boats and gear to dry for at least five days before using them in different waters, following Michigan’s guidance.

Never release aquarium pets, pond plants or unused bait into natural waters.
