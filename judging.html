Our Innovation Project

What we found

Our robotics community recognized some invasive species more often than others. Biologists helped us narrow our research to rusty crayfish and Asian longhorned ticks. In our survey, video was the most popular way to learn.

What we made and what we’ll test

We made a video, a website, a brochure, and 3D-printed outreach items. Our QR keychains take visitors to the website. We plan to see whether people can name one action after using our materials.

How we checked our tick research

Our student researcher looked at the tick’s size, how it reproduces, and when it was reported. We learned that its size depends on its life stage and whether it has fed. We also learned that females in U.S. populations can lay eggs without mating.

What the reports showed us

Our researcher wondered whether the tick arrived through a trade route. USDA’s records show an early U.S. detection in New Jersey in 2017, and Michigan reported its first detection in Berrien County in 2025. How it arrived is still a question for us to investigate.

Our Robot Design

How we planned and built Wildbot

We grouped nearby missions so one attachment could do more in a run. We built attachments on a common frame so we could swap them quickly, and we used My Blocks to reuse code.

What we changed after testing

Our first motor-powered Flip the Rock attachment worked inconsistently. We tried a simpler attachment, changed how it connected, and slowed Wildbot down. Testing those changes helped us get the mission working.
